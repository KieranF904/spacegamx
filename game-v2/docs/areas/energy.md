# Energy
How a ship makes, stores and spends power. **Every ship runs the ports core** (power/ports.ts, section "Ports core"
below): power crosses between parts only through their ports. A blueprint's `power` field is a marker kept from when
there were two models: a design with it or without it runs on the ports core (rebuild.ts), and the server still sets it
on every design a client sends (`clientBlueprint`). The pool and banks model (circuits.ts, wires.ts, the old energy.ts
tick, the hangar's POWER panel and the HUD's bank bars) was removed on 2026-11-10 with the pool-era designs; its design
history is docs/design/rework-2026-11.md §2 and docs/design/energy-system.md.

## Public interface
- `ShipDerived.ports: PortsLayout`, `ShipDerived.energy: EnergyInfo`.
- `Charge { e?: PortsState; row? }` (absent `e`: full, made on the first tick); `FULL_CHARGE` (`{}`); `fullPortsCharge(d)`; `powerTick(d, charge, PowerUse) -> PowerResult`; `remapCharge(before, after, c)` on every rebuild; `chargeTotal`; `ensurePortsCharge` (a client's state from its row).
- Wire: `packCharge`/`unpackCharge`; pilot row `data[8]` the layout's sig, `data[9]` minus the parts' triples' count, the triples from `CHARGE_GROUPS_AT` (13). A ship with no state yet sends one group (100). `CHARGE_GROUPS_LAYOUT` and `MAX_CHARGE_GROUPS` are in the wire hash (unchanged: changing them is a protocol bump). The gunSync message keeps the retired pool's leading slots as constants (otherGuns.ts packGunSync).

## Invariants
- Deterministic and identical on both sides: `powerTick` runs inside `stepFlight` for the server, the pilot's own tick layer (client OwnTick.step, common sim/ownStep.ts) and replicas. No Date.now/Math.random, no client/server imports. Per-tick scratch is module-level; a tick allocates only its result.

## How to extend
A new store, generator or consumer: models.mjs "ports energy core" block (`ports`, `profile`, `takes`, `feedPort`) and the ports core. A new power draw: make it a part feature (features/README.md) that asks through `PowerUse.extra` by part (a thruster with its own buffer, as the Burst Strafer and the Gyro).

## Tests
- `npx vitest run common/src/ships/power` (the ports core: layout, tick, features, machines, load test), `common/src/ships/features/strafer`, `features/gyro`, `client/src/ships/portsReport.test.ts`; goldens: `common/src/ships/golden` (flight traces), `server/src/golden`.

## Gotchas
- Still unaccounted on the ports core: the Tow Beam (its share is 1: Towing.ts), the Flash Screen (no store pays to bring it back at once: the pool's Capacitor-bank payment went with the pool), and a hazard's battery cap (HazardFlight.cap: radiation's `batteryCap` sets it, nothing reads it now).
- Fields kept for old data and tools, no longer read by the game: FunctionDef `pool`, `bank`, `dynamo`, `intake`, `discharge`, `carriesFlow`, `loss` (models.mjs writes them; the shipgen tools read some), tuning.json `energy`.

## Ports core (docs/design/energy-ports-2026-11.md §14)
`common/src/ships/power/ports.ts`: `buildPortsLayout` (rebuild.ts, every design: `ShipDerived.ports`), `portsTick` (energy.ts `powerTick` runs it; `Charge.e` is the `PortsState`), the guns' gates (`portsGunReady`, `portsSpendShot`, `portsBeamStep`: guns.ts), `portsSwitchFeeds` (the `feedSwitch` message, key Y), the wire (`packPorts`/`unpackPortsInto`: triples in the pilot row, counted negative in `data[9]`; `ensurePortsCharge` on the client). Data: models.mjs "ports energy core" block (`ports`, `profile`, `takes`, `feedPort`, the feed parts), tuning.json `feeds`, the core's ports in functions.json. Tests: `common/src/ships/power/ports.test.ts` on the dev ports ships; `models.test.ts` (every library ship ports, every consumer fed); `client/src/ships/portsReport.test.ts`.

**The switch (2026-11-05).** Engines take everything their lines bring (`rate` = their links' sum, not `intake`), and models.mjs rates an engine's in ports at its boosted burn, so a store or a strong line keeps a boost going and a weak one gives a dash from the buffer. Mining lasers draw 30 (Bore 20 a cell) and their ports carry it. Fusion Torch boost drain 0.4. Legacy functions (enemy designs) are touch-routed (`FunctionDef.touch`: gen, relay, in): they join whatever touches them, touching generators share their output by need, relays pass power only outward, the core is their hub, and an untouched consumer is wired to the nearest generator. `boostOf(d)` is a ports ship's boost for the autopilot (its `boostFactor` is 1). Strafers, gyros and shields draw (Kieran, 2026-11-06: "The strafe thruster should require power like all other thrusters, as should shields and gyros"; section "Strafers, gyros and shields" below). Still unaccounted on a ports ship: the Tow Beam and the Flash Screen (each a tool that draws its full rate every tick, but nothing it does depends on what it got), jump coils' feed changes nothing. The hangar shows a ports power report (portsReport.ts) and the energy overlay (docs/areas/hangar-ui.md): port states from `power/portStates.ts` (`portsView`: each port linked/open/wasted/clash/spare and every link; `previewPorts` for a part in hand) and the load test `power/portsLoadTest.ts` (`portsLoadTest(d, 'fire'|'boost'|'both', seconds)`: the real tick and guns' gates beside the same ship with unlimited power at its in ports, so each consumer's share is real/ideal work over a sliding window; brownout time, settled shares, stores, per-link E/s; test portsLoadTest.test.ts). ports.ts gives them `PortsLayout.intake` (a consumer's own intake before its lines cap it), `ports[].l` (link id), `portsSpace`, `portsLinkSend`.

**Strafers, gyros and shields (2026-11-06).** A Burst Strafer and a Gyro are ports thrusters (`P_THRUSTER`, `PortsLayout.thrustKind` `TK_STRAFER` / `TK_GYRO`; engines `TK_ENGINE`) with their own buffer (`buffer`, filled over their in ports at what their lines bring, as an engine) that burns `demand` a second only while their feature asks: the strafer's flight hook (features/strafer `dodgeLoad`) and the gyro's (features/gyro `gyroLoad`) put a per-part ask in `PowerUse.extra` on a ports ship (the share of each part's burn: a strafer by how much of its push goes the dodge's way, a gyro by how much of the ship's turning cap this tick's turn used), `portsPowerTick` marks them active with `PortsControls.scale`, and returns each feature's share (got/asked, weighted by burn) in `PowerResult.extra`, so the dodge pushes and the gyro's part of the turn stands by that share, (`DODGE_MIN_SHARE` refuses a burst that starts under a quarter), and replicas get it in the control bits (`ctlOfShare`). A strafer's 140 E buffer is one burst; after it the burst is only what its line refilled. No exhaust line-of-sight rule for strafers (mounting.ts `engineExhaust` is for `thrust` parts only; the strafer keeps its free-edge facing rule). A shield (not a Flash Screen) draws its `demand` every tick as a tool; `EnergyState.shieldSat` (the shields' fulfilment, by want) scales its regen: `shieldShare(d, charge)` in energy.ts, read by the server's shield phase (server/src/ships/bridge/flight.ts) and on a dormant ship's wake-up; an unfed shield runs its one-second buffer dry and then doesn't refill. And, as a jump coil (`portsFed`), a shield power can't reach at all through its links adds no capacity or regen (rebuild.ts; Flash Screens keep their capacity: they're left as they are). Totals for the hangar: `PortsLayout.strafeDraw`, `gyroDraw`, `shieldDraw` (portsReport `drawsText`: the POWER panel's Draws line), and buildFacts' "Draws ... during a burst / turning flat out / always" and Buffer rows. The load test's boost scenarios are combat flying: gyros turning flat out, strafers weaving a burst every cooldown (`PortsLoadResult.moves`). Test: `power/portsFeatures.test.ts`.

**Field machines (2026-11-07, docs/design/field-fabrication.md).** A fabricator or refiner (`FunctionDef.machine`) is a ports tool (`PortsLayout.machine` its kind, `MACHINE_FAB`/`MACHINE_REF`) that draws its full rate only while `PortsState.mach` has its bit (`setPortsMachine`: the server's decision, carried to the pilot by the own-ship sync) and nothing otherwise, competing for power like any other draw. What it got is summed in `PortsState.machEu[kind]` (`portsMachineEu`): its jobs progress by that energy (econ/field/jobs.ts). Both survive a remap into another design. `PortsLayout.machineDraw` totals them. Test: `power/portsMachines.test.ts`.

**The core as hub (Kieran, 2026-11-05).** Small library ships run their parts from the core: generators feed its two side inputs, and it feeds parts through its four outputs (directly or through conduits). The Sparrow T has two Cell Reactors into the core, Pips on its nose and Ion Drives under it. The Drum's core runs its Rattlers and Plasma Drive, and the Arrow's its Pips. Two limits shape these ships. A core side takes at most 60, so 120 into the core needs a ≥60 source on each side (an Overclock port or a Joiner Bank). And a rect2 core has only four outputs, so a fifth consumer needs a splitter or its own generator. The Prospector is the level-1 build tutorial and can only use level-0 parts, which pass power in straight lines (the Corner Conduit is level 2 and needs polymer). Its Pips and Ion Drives can't reach the core from the sides, so it keeps a reactor behind each part. Freighters run each Fusion Torch on an Overclock instead of a Fusion Plant (the Ark Tender keeps its centre plant), so boost is about 1.25x cruise. Player remakes for review (dev, `player-*.json`) use the same patterns.

## Shipgen's power model (scripts/shipgen; not the game's)
The ship generator's checks, legend and prompt rules (scripts/shipgen/core: energyLayout.ts, energyTick.ts,
energyState.ts, sim.ts, partPower.ts) run their own copy of the touch-routed power core (P1 of
docs/design/energy-routing-impl.md, which the game never ran: power moves between parts that share a full edge). It
moved out of common/src/ships/power on 2026-11-10. Retargeting shipgen at the ports core (and these rules with it) is open.

## Design rules (for ship designers; scripts/shipgen reads the block below into its prompt: keep it true to shipgen's power model)
<!-- shipgen:rules -->
- Power moves only between parts that share a full cell edge (they touch). A corner touch carries nothing. There are no wires.
- A GENERATOR pushes its power into the stores, conduits, diodes, the core and the consumers it touches. Nothing else makes power.
- A STORE (and the CORE) holds power and passes it to every part it touches: consumers, and other stores (touching stores even out). It gives at most its Gives a second and takes in at most its Takes a second.
- A CONDUIT is a power line: it passes power between the parts it touches, both ways, and holds very little. Use a row of them to reach parts far from the generators.
- A DIODE passes power one way: out of its marked side (^ > v <), into the part there, which it must touch. The parts touching its other sides can only feed it. Use it to keep a reserve (for engines or shields) that the guns can't drain.
- Hull, armour, cargo and every other part marked "carries no power" pass no power: a gun behind hull gets nothing.
- Every CONSUMER (guns, engines, shields, tools) must touch a generator, store, conduit, diode or the core, and that chain must lead back to a generator. A consumer drawing on a chain gets no more than its weakest link gives.
- Put generators beside the stores that feed the hungriest parts (big guns, boosting engines), and give each group of guns or engines its own store.
- Parts that face out (guns, engines, mining tools) need the edge they face left free: nothing across it. Engines usually face back (v); fixed guns face forward (^); turrets may face out at the sides.
- Every part must touch the ship by a full edge, part to part, back to the core. A ship has exactly one core.
<!-- /shipgen:rules -->
