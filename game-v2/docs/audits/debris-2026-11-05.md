# Deterministic debris (2026-11-05)

Debris is now simulated on every client's tick layer the way the server does it. After a piece spawns, nothing more is
sent about it unless something else touches it.

## What's built

- **Grace** (`DEBRIS_GRACE_TICKS` = 90, common ships/debris.ts): for 90 ticks after it spawns, a piece takes force only from its
  break-up. It neither knocks nor is knocked. Shots still hit it, because that's damage, not force. A first version also
  made young pieces immune to shots. That changed crowd fights: pieces stopped soaking shots, twice as many lay about,
  and capable bytes in ball 100 rose 17%. It was backed out. Sent pieces carry `born`/`aloof` in their first sync.
- **Life** (`CAPS.DEBRIS` 131072, protocol 44; common ships/debrisLife.ts, client ships/debris/DebrisLives.ts): each part's crumble
  tick is fixed at spawn. A client told `l` on the split runs the piece: crumbles, rebuilds, recentring, crumble effects,
  structure by tick for knocks, and further splits from the piece's body before it was recentred. Nothing about the piece
  is sent except its removal. A shot, fire or tow makes the piece *told* (`DebrisNet.told`): a sync goes out, and the
  server sends that piece's changes from then on.
- **STRUCTS clients** recentre sent pieces from their syncs (`rebuildAt`), so no debris rebuild costs them a keyframe. Legacy clients get
  keyframes for a recentred piece, for pieces within reach of it, and for pieces it knocked (`offLegacy`).
- **Sheds** (protocol 45): a boss's shed groups leave at the rebuild point as the first pieces of a split. Fling,
  crumble time and aloof travel in `g`. No boss needs server-driven pieces.
- **Split poses** (protocols 46, 47): splits worked out after the snapshot's decisions. A destroyed ship's split, or one of the
  client's own ship, carries the ship's pose (`s`). A split of a ship rebuilt since the client's last sync also carries the
  centre of mass and the pieces' HP (`c`, `h`).

## Practicality

The CPU cost is small, so no adaptive mode was needed. In the crowd ball there are 11–39 pieces on average (58 at most).
The server's debris step takes 0.01–0.04 ms a tick, and the probe client's debris world 0.01–0.05 ms.

## Known gaps

- **Ball 300:** 8 player tick-layer disagreements at one snapshot. This is round 4's timing-dependent cluster, and it is the same
  with the probes' DEBRIS bit off. Each one fails the held check and re-keys every held ship, which gives about 1,300
  keyframes. That re-key storm is behind ball 300's 25 corrections a second and its bytes.
- **Ball 50:** 7 own-ship snapshots off by one angle step, caught by the own check. The scene differs from the baseline
  run; I didn't chase it.
- **No check on run pieces after spawn:** the spawn check (ack) is the only check. A divergence would last until the piece is told,
  leaves view, or goes.
- **Shot before checked:** a piece shot before its ack is off until the told sync arrives (most of the ball's debris off ticks).
