# Lore canon (what the game's text says, October 2026)

Source of truth: the player-facing lore doc, "Player-Facing Lore: What the Cluster Believes" (Claude Doc `NcmnFC36qfqFVJHdcXjCv3`). It is everything players may learn. This file records what the game's text now says, so later text agrees with it. Keep the voice weathered and practical; short texts stay short. The secret canon never appears in game text.

## Rules for writing (quoted from the doc)
1. **Attribute everything.** A claim is always held by someone in the game, never stated by the narration.
2. **Never confirm or deny.** Questions about the lore are answered in character by someone who might be wrong, or not at all.
3. **Offer the ordinary explanation in the same breath.** Every strange event comes with a plain reading that someone defends.
4. **Nobody is right about the whole picture.** Sabine Kerr is the only character allowed to say she does not know.
5. **Keep events in their own records.** No codex entry, quest or NPC line connects two events unless a character in the game would plausibly connect them, and that character may be wrong.
6. **Nothing a player needs is hidden in a hint.**
7. **The cluster never speaks.** If anything ever does, it is once, in one line, and it says nothing about what it is.

Also: nothing says greyseam (or anything else) is a mind, aware, alive, kind or cruel (a named prospector's superstition aside). Nothing says where the heartbeat comes from.

## Names and terms
| Term | What the text says |
|---|---|
| the colony | Thousands of years old. Dating stays vague: "as long as there are records", "the early centuries". A human ship came through a gate that closed behind it; settlements disagree on its name and numbers. Text calls it "the first ship", "the crossing", "the first crews", "the colonists". It is never named and never called "the ark". |
| the Ark | An old order of duty founded in Sol, beginnings lost; about one in ten belong. Not a church: no gods, no miracles, service and discipline. The word is used for nothing else. "Herald" is the Ark's word too. See "The Ark" below. |
| leestone | Dense, dark, expensive stone, traces everywhere, richest in the Maw's shard ring. Only the Ark builds with it: "Ark stone". Nobody else sees the point. See "Leestone" below. |
| New Meridian / the Meridian Institute | The city on Terra Nova and Osei's institute. Nobody agrees where the name comes from; it's already on the oldest charts (Osei's topic "Where does New Meridian get its name?", the Terra Nova surface description). No link to any ship. |
| greyseam | Dull grey veins in near any rock; assays as a metal-silicate glass; refinery pulls it out. Goes into plate, shields, superconductors, sensor crystal, jump-drive lattices, and never fails in any of them. Mined, refined, installed; **no grades**. Some batches run better than others; technicians call it luck ("same batch"). Not an item of its own. |
| cores | The computing heart of ships, drones and stations: current driven through greyseam. Where the trouble is: unexplained failures, now and then an unexplained improvement, and **whiskering** (threads visible only under a microscope, found in many cores, usually harmless). Engineers blame architecture, firmware, manufacturers. Old pilots say "my ship is waking" when a core handles too well (Marrow: "it's settling in"). |
| Resonant Crystal | Glasswater's crystal, "the clearest greyseam anyone has found"; hums under a scanner. |
| Scrapborn | Feral machines that gather greyseam into nests (always built round greyseam). The colony treats them as a fault that happens to some cores, rarely; pilots have theories, technicians folklore; salvaged Scrapborn parts are said to be cursed (Achebe: "second-hand parts are second-hand"). Not anyone's old drones. |
| the Scrap Mother | Largest and oldest of the Scrapborn, nesting past Nereid in the Drift (south-west of Sol); her core has never been recovered ("went to pieces with the rest of her, same as every time"). |
| the heartbeat | Pilots' name for the signal. Recorded as long as records go back, never traced to a source. The observatory (Quinn): a natural effect, most likely of the Maw or the Glasswater pulsar; most physicists agree. Most of it is featureless hum; a handful of signatures are catalogued by number; **7-K**, strongest near Terra Nova, is the best known, and some read it as aimed at the planet (Quinn: "it's just the closest world to it"). "Signal peak" stays as the event name. |
| the Helios Directorate | Runs the cluster's infrastructure from the Helios Array; its working seat is the Helios Seat. The Helios Drones are the Directorate's Array drones (schedule nobody wrote). Its new projects keep failing and nobody can say why (Array description, Vance, Director Mbeki: "fourteen major works in eleven years"). Reputation `helios` "Helios Directorate". See "Bellwether" below. |
| Bellwether | The Directorate's answer: a signal array grown out of the Helios Array, sold as a shield for its work. The Director never says what it's against ("Against loss"; "a shield doesn't need a name for a thing to stop it"). Built on greyseam (scrap cores are the cheap feedstock) and power cells. Only the first act exists: Commissioning, the calibration pulse and anything about 7-K are not written yet. |
| the Gardener | Folk belief only, never a character: something tends the cluster, brings luck to the careful and cuts down the proud (the convoy that never came, the refinery that burned). Tales disagree where it lives: Sol's light, behind the closed gate, the Maw. Always voiced by someone who scoffs (Joe "Ever lost a cargo?", Sander "Do the ice crews have superstitions?"). |
| the Halden | An extinct people known from ruins; named for Halden, the larger moon of Cinderjack in Ashfall, where their first ruin was found. Halden Ring, the Helios Array's oldest arc (segments 41-49), sealed cases (Ancient Relics: "Halden work, collectors say, even the ones that turn up in the Maw"). |
| the New Halden | A human faith: a god (Aske: "the sleeper in the stone") sleeps in the stone around Sol; the Halden were its priests. Preachers at Tinder Claim; Brother Aske on Glacia. |
| the Maw people | Known only from wreckage and badly translated etched plates that disagree about how they ended (a sickness in their machines, a war, a failing star, a god arriving). Nothing else they built still works (Kask). |
| Vesper Station | The buyer in the Hollow Crown's ledger. It doesn't exist ("a pirate scam, likely"). |
| Stillstones | Huge bodies of dark stone (ids still `*_lodestone_*`), each with one great carving across its face, worn at the rim: a crown with needles, a rosette, or nested polygons. Jump drives work better near them; nobody knows why. Sol has four live ones plus a scarred one in the Drift; Ashfall, Borealis (with the small rough Aurora Knot) and Glasswater three; Nebula Prime two; Cinder Reach one live, one scarred, one dead; the Void a scarred one and a shattered one; the Maw only dead and shattered. Each has an attributed description (who built on it, what went dark, what the Guild says); nobody in the text knows why they work or fail. Okafor's topic "What are the stillstones?" and Sander's ice_02 line give the public facts; the Aurora Knot is a small rough one charted a few years back. |

The Drowned Beacon (item id `meridian_beacon`) in the Pale Reef is older than any record; the Museum Guild argues whose landing it marks (job tn_beacon).
- **Ashfall Drift**: the Halden's home. The brown dwarf's ignition scar. The Halden Ring: "The Halden built it, and stopped." Redtooth dig under Halden's crust; the Redtooth Hold is stacked with small sealed crates.
- **Glasswater**: the pulsar's beat "hasn't slipped a tick" in all the records anyone keeps. Prism's crystal is greyseam at its clearest and rings on the heartbeat's note. main_05: the old crystal hands say something past the Maw is broadcasting and the cluster is the bell; Quinn says it's the pulsar setting the crystal off. - **Borealis**: the aurora masks the signal; the Brood nest in the Curtain and scatter at peaks (Maya: animal behaviour). main_04: Maya relays the observatory's natural-effect reading and wonders why the Brood hate it. The **Lighter Halvard**: an early colony lander lost on the ice road in the early centuries; its log says Rimeholt's bastion was "standing, lit, and nobody home", and its nav core came off the same line as the first crews' own and flew "just like the old girl" (her mothership, Maya supposes: "same batch"). **Rimeholt**: its bastion is older than the colony; ice crews have sheltered by it as long as there have been ice crews. **Glacia**: Halden site, Brother Aske. The Cult: the furnace hum is a summons from the Maw calling the worthy home; they burn other ships flying Maw-ward.
- **Void Sector**: debris thrown from the Maw; the heartbeat echoes there; wraiths condense. The Void Leviathan: "Where the heartbeat echoes". Its death scatters the wraiths; no pause in the heartbeat.
- **The Maw**: the shard ring was a world with people; the Watch buys their writing (Kask, above). Heart of the Maw: "Where the heartbeat is loudest", looks grown out of the shard ring's rock. Its death (main_13): pilots across the cluster report the heartbeat falling silent for a moment, then resuming; the observatory recorded nothing unusual; Kerr says it changed and doesn't know what that means.

## The signal (the heartbeat)
- Kerr: forty years of notebooks plus older records; it drifts; it has paused before; greyseam has a faint hum of its own, the same shape, which she thinks is related; she's not sure what any of it means. She lost about six years of her record (the hab's drives froze one winter); Quinn loves pointing that out (Kerr topic "Dr. Quinn says your records have a gap.").
- Quinn: natural effect (Maw or pulsar); the catalogue and 7-K; Kerr's drift is her gear picking up the heartbeat itself. Pass 2: the Rustjaw relay spike with nothing weakened after (equipment); Kerr's array recording is instrument drift; her filaments are electrochemistry.
- Kerr, pass 2: her recording of the Helios Array (bw_03, record `kerr_array_recording`): a faint shape that creeps in as the recording runs; "I'll say what I hear. That's all I'll say." Her dish (kerr_03, `kerr_filaments`): see "Sabine's dish".

## The Ark
- **What the text says**: an order of duty, not a church (Keeper: "No gods, no miracles. Service and discipline."); founded in Sol so long ago "nobody has the founding on file"; about one in ten belong, from a bag by the door to the devoted. Reputation `ark` "The Ark" (quests.json; quartermaster at the Hall: kits and bonds at rank 0, field kits, scanner charges and dressed leestone at 1, leestone plate and servo motors at 2, the leestone schematic at 3).
- **Heralds**: arrive in grey, say "You must leave", give a time, go. Never a reason. The herald at the Hall greets everyone "You must leave."; "When?" → "Nine hours."; "Why?" → "I carry the word." Nothing else, ever. Nobody (the Keeper included) says how heralds know: the Keeper's line is about his job, "The word comes down. I keep the books." His ledger has a second line for the day each word came true, and he's "never had to leave the second line blank" (the Directorate's auditors: you only write down the ones that land). Achebe: "They've never been wrong. I hate it." (her plain reading: listening posts the Patrol lacks).
- **Practices and their stated reasons** (always the members' own explanations, never the narration's): bag by the door and leave when told ("just sense"); short tenures ("complacency kills; a crew that stays too long loses its edge"); homesteads spread apart ("small towns evacuate faster"); homes built light ("easier to leave"); the core regimen ("discipline, and pride in one's equipment"); old machinery retired early and scattered ("accountability and inventory control"); nothing cycled needlessly ("energy discipline"); leestone ("tradition, and durability").
- **Outsiders**: Marrow, "Ark-heavy" (leestone plate near twice titanium's weight, no better when hit) and "polishing the grey" (their ships fly no better; "a lot of spanner time"). Joe, the cynic: the heralds always come right before the disaster, so whoever knows when the roof comes in may be holding it (Achebe's reading: better listening posts). Aske, New Halden: the Ark keeps their god "in a grey box and calls it inventory" (the Keeper: stone and ledgers, "I've done the inventory"). The relic: people assume it from the name; the Ark has never said it keeps anything but stone and ledgers (Keeper topic). The Directorate: secular rival ("They run the lights. We were here before the lights."). Kask's crews call the Maw expeditions "stone-eaters", dying over a rock.
- **Depots burn**: the Ark Depot is ordinary stone and has burned twice (Marek; inspectors: ordinary fires); sceptics' favourite argument. Nobody hurt either time, "the stock was mostly out" (said, not explained).
- **Never written**: how heralds know; anything about a relic beyond the rumour; any narrated link between heralds and the Gardener; leestone doing anything measurable; greyseam aware.

## Visual language
- Halden work: dark slate faces, darker edges, notched never-square corners, a thin cold greyseam inlay (client/src/rendering/Scenery.ts `HALDEN`).
- Greyseam in rock: thin dull grey veins with a faint self-glow on about a third of non-ice asteroids (AsteroidLook.ts).
- Stillstones: dark rock in nearly regular cut faces, glassy and iridescent where fractured; one great carving whose grooves repeat at every scale (crown with a pit and needles past the edge; rosette of petalled terraces; nested polygons); faint cold light in the grooves and thin cracks, most on the night side; warm and rough on the few new ones; scarred, dead and shattered states. Never glyphs or writing.
