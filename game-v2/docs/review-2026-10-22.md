# Project review, 22 October 2026

Five read-only passes over the code and data: server performance, architecture and data-driven design, progression and economy, client netcode and UI, and rendering. Every finding below was checked against the code; where a reviewer's claim didn't hold up it's noted rather than silently dropped. Balance numbers are paper estimates from the data files, not play measurements.

Baseline: ~70k lines of TypeScript (client 39k, server 16k, common 16k), 28 test files, 223 KB of JSON data. The server tick runs at ~1.8 ms in heavy combat after this week's collision and asteroid work, so performance is now headroom work, not firefighting. The bigger issues are in progression gating and in two legacy systems that were never deleted.

## 2. Server performance

The tick is 1.8 ms in an 8-pilot fight. These are the remaining costs, ranked. None change gameplay results unless marked.

1. **Every ship body is simulated twice per tick in every system, including the ~11 empty ones.** `ShipBridge.tick` steps all bodies; `Replication.endTick` then re-steps a full replica of each to detect divergence, calling `host.env()` (allocates) and `host.dockOf()` → `liveConn` (linear scan of clients) per body. The 26 permanent bodies (derelicts, traffic, person craft) are a fixed floor. Fix: build `occupiedSystems` once per tick; skip replica stepping for unoccupied systems (set `diverged = true` so a keyframe goes out when someone arrives); build `dockedBy` once per tick. Effort S. Skipping flight stepping too is M and would pause traffic in unobserved systems.
2. **Beams and fused shells scan every body in the world.** `stepMiners`, `fireBeam` and `nearestTarget` (flak/EMP every tick in flight) loop all `bodies`, calling `isJumping` (the `jumpTuning()` spread) and `canDamage` (4 lookups) *before* the bounding-circle reject. Fix: expose the spatial grid to the bridge through `ShipHost`, query the beam segment / fuse radius, and test the circle first. Effort M.
3. **`LootPods.tick` is O(pods × clients × cargo).** Per pod it builds the collector list (scans clients), then per player does `connOf` (linear) and `cargoUsed`. Mining throws pods constantly. Fix: collectors by system with `cargoFree` computed once per tick; distance test first. Effort S.
4. **Per-client snapshot bookkeeping is O(clients × bodies) at 20 Hz.** `updateShipView` loops every body in the world per client; `sendShipUpdates` recomputes `hpWire` for every part of every near ship per client (10 clients × 30 ships × 40 parts = 12k calls per snapshot). Fix: group bodies by system once; compute each ship's wire-HP once per snapshot, bumped by an `hpVersion` on damage. Effort M.
5. **`broadcastToSystem` rescans all clients per event.** Combat produces hundreds of events a second. Fix: `clientsBySystem` built once per tick. Effort S.
6. **Allocation inventory in `ShipBridge.tick`:** `{...K, gravity}` per body per tick (cache per system); `readBody`/`writeBody` 2–3 object allocs per body; `new Set()` + two arrays per armed ship even when idle; `[...bodies.values()].filter()` just to count debris. Effort S each.
7. **Legacy enemy branches in `collisionSystem`** still cost 2–4 `hasComponent` per projectile per tick for an always-empty set. Delete with §3.1.
8. **Stale maps** (finding 6 above), plus `contactFx` cleanup is an O(all keys) string scan per body removal: key by numeric pair.
9. **Small 60 Hz loops that could be 10 Hz:** `Pilots.tick` station-range check, `riftSystem` portal scan, `trafficSystem` station lookup, `recordSelfHashes` string hashing, and the `[DRAIN]` console.log (stdout to a pipe is synchronous on Linux and blocks the tick).
10. Known from the earlier profile: `jumpTuning()` spread (2%), `partDeaths` full scan (5%), `systemRows` JSON.stringify keys (6%).

Together, 1–5 and 10 are roughly 25–30% of the current tick. The asteroid death scan I left in this week is ~2 µs and not worth touching.

## 3. Architecture and data-driven design

### 3.1 Delete the legacy enemy/NPC stack (M, low risk, 700–900 lines)
`enemyAISystem`, `enemyAttack`, `enemySpawnSystem`, `spawnEnemy` (`GameServer.ts:3357-3568`) are never called. But `collisionSystem`, `buildSpatialGrid`, `systemRows` and two admin methods still carry their branches; `data/enemies.json` and `npcs.json` are still loaded and bundled; `systems.json` still has `enemies`/`npcs` arrays nothing reads; `handleInteract` still contains the pre-People station menu ("Access Bank / Repair Ship…") and NPC "Heal me" dialogue; `createNPC` still spawns those entities; `constants.ts` has 11 `ICE_SPRITE_*` and ~30 other constants with zero importers; `common/src/networking/*` (~2k lines) is only used by `sandbox.ts`; `schemas.ts` still defines `ItemData` fields no item has and a `QuestData` that isn't the real quest schema. The client mirrors it: `EntityType.Enemy` cases in Renderer, TargetUI, shipReadout, Game.ts; `InventoryUI.ts` (494 lines) has no importer; `DebugUI` is constructed every session and never shown; `LoginUI` keeps a second WebGL context and Pixi ticker rendering a hidden canvas for the whole play session.

Doing this first makes everything else simpler and removes the `Health` double-ownership (next).

### 3.2 One owner per concept (S–M)
`Health` is written by `ShipBridge.syncHealth` and `createPlayer` (`PLAYER_MAX_HP = 100`) and read for death by `collisionSystem`; `Boost.fuel` mirrors `chargeTotal()`; progression lives in `GameState`, `PilotState.profile` and the entity row on the client, and different code picks different ones. Make `ShipBridge` the single owner of ship health (`coreHp`, `isDestroyed`), keep `Health` for asteroids only; `PilotState` owns progression on the client.

### 3.3 Validate data at load (M, no risk, highest leverage)
Nothing validates any JSON file. A quest step naming a non-existent station, person, boss, plan or item; a `people.json` station that doesn't exist; a typo in `factions.json spawns`; a boss `design` file missing; a `tuning.weapons` kind missing from `WEAPON_KIND_TYPE`/`DICTIONARY` — all load fine and fail at runtime or never trigger. `GameDataFiles.factions/bosses/achievements` are typed `unknown`. Add `validateGameData.ts`: shape check against the existing TS interfaces (hand-written or zod), then a cross-reference pass over every id. Fail fast in dev, warn in prod, and run it as a test. This is what turns "add a quest" into a one-file change you can trust.

### 3.4 A new weapon kind touches ~12 code files (M)
`FN_NAMES`, `FN_RARITY`, `fnDescription`, `WEAPON_KIND_TYPE`, `DICTIONARY`, `WEAPON_FNS`, `shipColors`, `partDecor` (19 cases), `DamageFx` (14 cases), `fieldGuide`, `StationWindow FAB_CATS`, `AudioManager` voice tables, plus the server verb. Behaviour is already data-driven in `tuning.weapons`. Put the presentation in `functions.json` (`name`, `rarity`, `description`, `category`, `color`, `decor` key, `sfx`, `wireCode`) and derive `DICTIONARY`'s part/material/piece sections from the catalog at startup on both ends. New part → 0–1 code files.

### 3.5 Tuning tables living in code (M, many small moves)
`Sieges.ts` (roster, turrets, repair, scrap-back, wave budget), `Surfaces.ts` (ranges, timers, wave size, rewards), `Events.ts` (roll/cooldown/chance, raid rings, rewards, two banners), `Operations.ts` (scan/outpost/escort distances, wave gaps), `Enemies.fly()` (~15 AI knobs: think interval, patrol hop, strafe flip, aim error, back-off, assist share), `Bosses.ts` (escort ring, blink range, nova lead), `ShipBridge.ts` (EMP immunity, wreck pattern, gunnery bonus, missile lock, beam defaults), `Pilots.ts` (cargo/nav, engineering discount), `GameServer.createQuestHost` bounty ore lists by danger (duplicates `mining.json`). Each has an obvious home in an existing data file or one `data/rules.json`. Keep the code values as defaults.

### 3.6 Player-facing text in code (S)
Vance's welcome call (`Quests.ts:310`), 24 stock replies in `People.ts`, event/op/siege banners, "You have been destroyed!", chat help. `people.json`/`quests.json` already carry a full dialogue DSL. A `data/strings.json` with `{name}` placeholders lets text change without a rebuild.

### 3.7 Two siege engines
`Surfaces.ts` (city siege: pylons, waves) and `Sieges.ts` (moon siege: bastion, turrets, day/night) are separate state machines with separate wave builders and reward formulas; the city one has no siege configured anywhere. One `Siege` definition in data with `Sieges.ts` as the interpreter.

### 3.8 Boss phases and enemy behaviour
Phase verbs (`say/escorts/enrage/heal/blink/nova`) are good data but each is an `if` in two places; dispatch through a `Record<op, fn>` so new ops are one entry. Enemy `Behaviour` has 6 numeric knobs and no way to express "kite", "ram", "guard X"; add `mode` and branch once in `fly()`.

### 3.9 The god files (L, do last)
`GameServer.ts` (4,837 lines) is four things: world construction (`:1798-2080`), a 58-case message switch, the whole outbound net layer (`:4037-4636`), and admin/stats. Sixteen methods still take `any` where typed schemas exist; host factories are re-instantiated (`createSurfaceHost` three times). `Game.ts` (2,120) has a 371-line `handleServerMessage` switch and a fixed-order modal key stack. `ShipBridge.ts` (1,663) is a second simulation kernel with a 17-optional-method host. Split along the seams that already exist, as pure moves, with the deterministic-fight hash as the safety net. Not urgent; everything above is worth more per hour.

### 3.10 UI kit
40 UI files, 30 with their own `<style>`; `windowKit` used by 9. `StationWindow` and `GameMenuUI` each have their own tab implementation. Add `tabs()`, `list()`, a shared `theme.css`, and a small `h()` element helper (also closes the `innerHTML` with server strings surface in QuestUI/SocialUI). Migrate the two tab implementations first.

### 3.11 Smaller
- Missing `ServerMessage` types (`effect`, `systemMessage`, `authResult`, `adminStats`) are why every production `as any` in GameServer exists. Add them.
- `MusicEngine.ts` hardcodes a mood per system id; `systems.json visuals` is the home.
- 16 client files import `data/*.json` directly, so a data change needs a client rebuild and versions can disagree. Short term: a data-version hash in `welcome`. Long term: serve `/data/*.json` with ETags.
- Reconnect: `connect()` never closes the previous socket and races the manual overlay; a reconnect is a full rejoin that kills the lingering ship. Resume-by-token (rebind `conn.playerId` to the lingering entity) would make a 2 s Wi-Fi blip survivable.
- `SHIP_VIEW_IN/OUT = 40/48 km` vs max zoom ≈ 24 km: every ship 24–40 km away still costs rows per snapshot.
- Bandwidth is fine: ~2–3 KB/s per pilot deflated in an 8-pilot fight. CPU in snapshot building will matter before bandwidth.

## 4. Progression, economy and balance

### 4.1 The level curve vs what the content pays
XP to level is `75·(L−1)³`. All designed content done once (every quest chain 62k, every boss 28k, ribbons ~6k) reaches about level 11.5.

| Level | XP needed | Gated here | Quest XP reachable by then |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 600 | drift_03, osei_01, wren_01 | ~1,200 |
| 5 | 4,800 | main_04, osei_03 (steel) | ~12,000 (all chains) / **3,600 story-only** |
| 8 | 25,725 | main_07 | 22,050 |
| 9 | 38,400 | main_08 (Rustjaw) | 29,550 |
| 11 | 75,000 | main_09 (titanium) | 32,750 |
| 12 | 99,825 | main_10 (Maw licence) | 36,750 |
| 15 | 205,800 | main_13, Mk IV licence | 60,750 |
| 20 | 514,425 | Mk V licence | — |

Findings:
- **Story-only players hit a wall at clearance.** main_01–03 + clearance = 1,200 XP (level 3); main_04 needs level 5 (4,800). Side chains cover it, but "nothing is forced" means a player following Vance's calls grinds ~80 Drift packs. Fix: main_04 at level 4, main_05 at 5; or clearance pays 1,500 (three buoys, a quiz and two talks for 350 XP is the lowest XP per step in the data).
- **The level 9→11 wall before main_09.** ~41k reachable, 75k needed; the only Mk II content paying story-relevant XP is Nebula/Borealis at 10–20k/h, so 2–3 hours of grinding with nothing new unlocking. After that, Cinder (25–40k/h) and the Maw (60–100k/h) outrun the cubic curve: L11→15 in ~3 h, so Mk IV arrives about an hour after the player first builds Mk III, and a titanium ship (31k cr, 3–5 h to fabricate) is obsolete before it finishes. Fix: either flatten to `75·(L−1)^2.6` (L11 = 30k, L15 = 72k, L20 = 160k), or raise main_08–13 XP ×3 and put main_09 at level 10. Also raise Mk II kill pay (`gradeReward` exponent 1.6 → 2.0).
- Estimated solo hours (all chains, no boss farming): L5 ≈ 2 h, L8 ≈ 4 h, L11 ≈ 8 h, L15 ≈ 12 h, L20 ≈ 16 h.

### 4.2 Credits
Income per hour (paper): Sol Drift kills ~2.5k; Glasswater crystal mining with a Prospector 8–12k at level 3; Borealis Mk II kills 2–4k; Cinder 8–12k; Maw 20–30k; boss farming 8.6k (Scrap Mother) to 65k (Maw Heart). Quest pay for the entire game: 12.2k. Costs: Prospector 452 cr, Corsair Mk II ~1.5k, Lancer Mk III 31k, Galleon Mk III 51k, Bastion Mk V 575k. Sinks: effectively none (§1 items 4, 5).

- **Mining out-earns combat until Cinder** because kill credits in Mk I–II systems are 2–24 cr per ship. Either kill credits ×3 there (skiff 9→25, raider 24→70) or lower `tools.miner.yield` so haul size matters.
- **Trade chain and supply bounties are satisfiable from station stock.** trade_03 asks for titanium plate "from the Ember Forge, if you can live through Cinder Reach": Sol Trade Hub sells it for 33 cr, 330 cr in → 750 cr out. Fix: make the deliver steps refine/craft-sourced, or stop Sol selling steel/titanium plate (which also closes §1 item 1's cheapest path).
- **Quest rewards don't track step count.** osei_02 (6 steps, 2 systems, a craft, two descents) pays 60 cr; the whole science chain pays 190 cr for 12 steps; single-step main_06 pays 225, guild_05 330 + 2,000 XP + EMP + skill point; kerr_01 (3 far visits) 50 cr. Rule of thumb: `credits ≈ 40 × steps × level`, `xp ≈ 60 × steps × level^1.5`. Give osei_03 a steel starter kit (steel_plate 6 → 30) so steel is usable the moment it's learned.

### 4.3 Combat and ships
- **Steel is a mobility trap** (density 1.6). Regrading to Mk II roughly halves cruise speed: Bulwark 402→287, Corsair 294→190, Galleon 65→37, Hammerhead 34→**16 u/s**. Enemies are regraded the same way, but a capital ship at 16–37 u/s can't chase anything and needs 30+ min to cross a lane without chaining jumps. Fix: steel density 1.25, or scale `partThrust` by output^0.75.
- **Story order inverts difficulty at the end.** main_11 (L13) fights the Maw (Mk V, avg pack 85); main_12 (L14) goes to the Void (Mk IV, pack 53), reachable only through the Maw; main_13 returns. Leviathan is weaker relative to a Galleon than the Cinder Warden's escorts. Fix: swap main_11/main_12 content, or regrade the Void to 5, and give the Void its own entrance (a Nebula rift) so wraith schematic drops aren't locked behind main_10.
- **Rustjaw is the hardest fight relative to gear and sits on the level 9 gate.** Its phase escorts are full-strength Mk II (2 raiders at 229 dps + a gunship at 407) plus a 12% heal; a Corsair Mk II dies on paper in ~5 s while needing 13 s on the boss alone. Hoarfrost by contrast does 22 dps and can't kill a Bulwark in a minute. Fix: apply the boss `damage` factor to spawned escorts (or ×0.5), Hoarfrost damage 0.4→1.0, Rustjaw 0.15→0.3 with the gunship escort replaced by a raider.
- **Mk IV and Mk V licences have no deterministic source.** `schematic_cobalt` is 0.8–1.2% drops, `schematic_neutronium` 0.5–1%; no vendor stocks them. Expected 85–200 kills with no pity. Put cobalt on the Miners' Guild vendor at rank 4 and neutronium on the Shipwrights at rank 5, or as rewards on main_11/main_13.
- The Arrow (main_03, "a proper fighter") rates 11.0, the same as the starting Sparrow (10.2) and with less cargo than the Prospector. Give it a third cannon or a steel core.
- Fabrication time is the real late-game gate and isn't surfaced: Galleon Mk III 5 h, Hammerhead Mk III 6.5 h, Bastion Mk V 16.7 h, serial per station, and a Mk V Bastion needs ~6,000 neutronium ore (the Maw has ~78 mega rocks per 5 min). Parallelise across `queueSlots`, cap `timeByGrade` at 3, let Horizon Watch sell neutronium plate at rank 5.

### 4.4 Quest flow and playability
- Boss quest steps block on the shared respawn with no feedback: if someone else killed Rustjaw, the player waits up to 10 min at an empty lair. Send `nextSpawn` in `bossLair` and show "returns in m:ss" on the marker, or instance story bosses per party like the Foundry Warden.
- `wren_01` has no starter, call or reveal: found only by flying within 900 u of (41000, 15000) in Glasswater. Give Orrin or Pell a topic revealing it. `patrol_01` is `chain: "traders"` but is Achebe's and pays Sol Patrol rep.
- The objective guide routes through locked gates (`route.ts exits()` ignores `restricted`), so an unlicensed player is pointed at a gate that refuses them. Pass licences to `routeStep` and label barred exits.
- `schematic_steel` (700 cr) and `schematic_composite` exist as items but nothing sells or needs them (composite structure is placeable without it); they show in tooltips.
- Skill points: 1/level + 10 from quests; 4 skills × 10 cap = 40 at ~level 30; levels 31–50 give nothing.
- Consistent and fine: mining hardness tiers, reputation ranks vs vendor stock (except the Mk IV/V gap), market saturation, respawn timers, travel times for Mk I hulls (Sol is 120k across, ~5 min for the clearance loop in a Sparrow).

## 5. Graphics polish, ranked by impact ÷ cost

The client already has a lot: biome sky with six parallax star layers, fBM sun with LOD, corona streamers, black hole and pulsar shaders, planets with terminators and atmosphere, per-vertex lighting with 16 lights, batched asteroids, quarter-res bloom, pooled sprite effects for tracers/flashes/sparks/smoke, thruster plumes with shock diamonds, part-specific destruction, shield ripple, jump streaks, screen shake, damage wedges, bitmap damage numbers. The wins below are mostly fixing what's there.

1. **Glow time bug, frame-rate dependence** (§1 items 7–8). Zero cost, the sun comes alive.
2. **Minimap hostiles, gate transit flashes** (§1 items 9–10). S.
3. **Name tags, health bars and damage numbers are world-space:** ~3 px at zoom 0.3, blurry 2× raster at zoom 2. Counter-scale by `clamp(1/zoom, 1, 3)` where positioned (`Renderer.ts:818-841`, `ShipRenderer.ts:268-292`) and install the damage-number font at resolution 2. S/M.
4. **Screen shake is per-frame white noise and stronger hits cancel weaker ones.** Replace with trauma: `trauma = min(1, trauma + k)`, offset = `trauma² · amp · noise(25 Hz)`, plus a little `worldContainer.rotation`. Big feel improvement, nil cost. S.
5. **Pop-in at the cull edge and on spawn/despawn.** The cull tests the centre with a 200 u margin; stations are 150–320 radius, so their edge pops at the screen border. Use per-type radius; fade alpha on spawn/despawn (the `dyingEntities` map was meant for this). S.
6. **Death screen:** bare text at alpha 0.7, instantly, no killer name though `showDeathScreen(killerName?)` is declared. Tween alpha and scale, show the killer, slow the camera follow while dead. S.
7. **Jump gates are static discs.** Slow counter-rotating rings with an alpha pulse, or instantiate `RiftShader` at the gate's hue with a lower swirl; pulse the light they already emit in step. S.
8. **Effect textures have no mipmaps:** sparks and plumes shimmer when zoomed out. `autoGenerateMipmaps: true` in `fxTextures.paint()`. S.
9. **Thruster plumes are a fixed blue for every ship;** tint `plumeBody` by faction/paint hue at no cost. S.
10. **Health bar scales about the wrong origin** (shrinks from both ends) and the player bar lacks the shield strip and red/amber steps the hostile bar has. S.
11. **Per-frame waste in the background path:** `systems.find` twice per frame, `buildStyleParams` allocating twice, corona rebuilding an RNG and options object every frame, `SunRenderer.update` allocating ~15 `Float32Array`s per frame (forcing uniform re-uploads), five `getChildByName` scans per entity per frame. Cache per system id. S, small CPU win.
12. **~330 lines of dead legacy effect code** (`updateFireEffects`, `drawFireEffect`, `createEnemyGraphics`, `createRockShape`, `StarfieldRenderer`, `NebulaRenderer`, `particles/`, `trails/`) only reachable from the sandbox or nothing. The tractor beam is the last live per-frame `Graphics` redraw; port it to two pooled streak sprites.
